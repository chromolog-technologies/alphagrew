AI Interviewer In current build

Practise the interview your resume will get you.

Students upload a resume, choose the role they want and answer questions about their own projects. When an answer is thin, the interviewer asks the follow-up a real panel would, then explains what a stronger answer includes.

Book a demoSee a sample report

Why it helps

Interview practice for every student, not only the confident few

  • 01

    Practice that starts from the student

    Questions draw on the projects and skills on the student's own resume, not a generic list.

  • 02

    A second question when it matters

    A vague answer or an unchecked claim gets one follow-up, the way a panel would probe.

  • 03

    Feedback with reasons

    Five rubric areas, each explained, with strengths, gaps and what a stronger answer includes.

  • 04

    Coaching where it is needed

    Results help the placement cell see who needs support before a company's interview day.

What's real today

  • Typed mock interviews with resume and role context, five interview types and follow-up questions are in the current build.
  • Feedback reports, resume parsing and skill gaps are in development. Speech is in development; there is no video.
  • Tested end to end with the built-in question bank and rule-based scoring. Live AI provider quality is still being evaluated.

How it works

From resume to feedback in six steps

Each step shows its build status. What a student sees depends on the features their institution has turned on.

  1. 1

    Student

    Upload a resume

    A PDF or Word file, stored privately. If a file cannot be read, the student is told why.
    In current build
  2. 2

    AlphaGrew

    Read skills, projects and experience

    The resume is parsed into skills, projects, experience and education. A screen to check and correct what was read is planned.
    In development
  3. 3

    Student

    Choose a role and interview type

    A target role or a campus job, one of five interview types, experience level, starting difficulty and a time limit.
    In current build
  4. 4

    AlphaGrew

    Plan the questions

    The interview is planned from the role and the resume. Without an AI provider configured, questions come from a curated question bank.
    In current build
  5. 5

    Student

    Answer in writing

    One question at a time, typed. Difficulty moves one step between questions by a fixed rule, and a weak answer can get one follow-up.
    In current build
  6. 6

    AlphaGrew

    Show feedback and next steps

    A report with rubric scores, strengths, areas to improve and per-question notes, plus a skill-gap view for the role.
    In development

Example questions

What students are asked

Examples in the style of each interview type. Live questions depend on the resume, the role and the current difficulty.

Fundamentals for the target role, from easier to harder.

  1. Difficulty 1

    “What is the difference between an array and a linked list, and when would you choose each?”

    Checks core data-structure reasoning.

  2. Difficulty 3

    “What is the difference between PUT and PATCH, and when would you use each?”

    Common in backend and API roles.

  3. Difficulty 5

    “How would you design an LRU cache with O(1) get and put operations?”

    Tests combining structures under constraints.

Motivation, teamwork and how the student talks about their choices.

  1. Opening

    “Tell me about yourself in two minutes, focused on why this role.”

    Practises a structured, relevant introduction.

  2. Behavioural

    “Describe a time a project teammate missed a deadline. What did you do?”

    Looks for ownership and a clear outcome.

  3. Closing

    “Where do you want to be two years after joining?”

    Checks the answer fits the role on offer.

Built from the student's own projects and experience.

  1. Project

    “Your complaint tracker uses PostgreSQL. How would you make the daily hostel-and-status query fast?”

    Tests whether the student understands their own design.

  2. Claim check

    “You list Spring Boot. Walk me through what happens when a request reaches your controller.”

    A listed skill is probed before it counts.

  3. Internship

    “What did you change during your internship, and how did you know it worked?”

    Looks for specific, measured impact.

Open-ended design with trade-offs, for roles that need it.

  1. Difficulty 3

    “How would you make a payment-creation endpoint safe to retry?”

    Idempotency and failure handling.

  2. Difficulty 4

    “How does a B-Tree index speed up a query, and what does it cost on writes?”

    Storage trade-offs behind everyday decisions.

  3. Difficulty 5

    “Design a notification service for drive deadlines across many institutions.”

    Scale, isolation and delivery guarantees.

A blend of technical, resume and HR questions, closest to a full campus round.

  1. Technical

    “What is a database transaction, and why does isolation matter?”

    Fundamentals first.

  2. Resume

    “Why did you choose MQTT over plain HTTP for your soil monitor?”

    Connects theory to their own project.

  3. HR

    “Why this company, and why now?”

    Ends the round as a real panel would.

After the interview In development

A report a student can act on

Each interview ends with scores in five weighted areas, what went well, what to work on and a note on each question. The scores are a practice measure for the student, not a verdict on them.

  • Weights are fixed and shown: technical depth 30%, problem solving 25%, communication 20%, role fit 15%, behavioural 10%.
  • With an AI provider, the evaluation is written by the model. Without one, it is built from per-answer signals.
  • Staff review and correction of AI feedback is planned.

Who benefits: Students

Five interview types

Technical, HR, resume deep dive, system design or mixed, with experience level and a time limit.

Who benefits: Students, Faculty

Trainers can point students to the round they find hardest.

In current build

Follow-up questions

If an answer is too vague or a claim needs checking, the interviewer asks one follow-up, or steps back to fundamentals.

Who benefits: Students

Students learn to give complete answers the first time.

In current build

Difficulty that steps up or down

Between questions, a fixed rule lowers difficulty after a weak answer and raises it after two strong ones, on a scale of 1 to 5. It is a rule, not live AI judgement.

Who benefits: Students

Nobody is stuck on questions far above or below their level.

In development

Feedback report

Technical depth, problem solving, communication, role fit and behavioural answers, with strengths, weaknesses and suggestions.

Who benefits: Students, Faculty

Students know what to fix; mentors know what to coach.

In development

Skill gaps after each interview

Interview results feed the skill-gap view for the target role, with suggested practice steps.

Who benefits: Students, Placement Officers

Practice turns into a plan rather than a one-off score.

In current build

Interview history

Past interviews stay listed with their reports, so a student can compare one attempt with the next.

Who benefits: Students, Faculty

Progress is visible, which keeps students practising.

In development

Spoken answers

Speech transcription exists on the server but is not yet part of the interview screen. There is no video interview.

Who benefits: Students

Will let students practise speaking, not just writing, once released.

Guardrails

What the AI Interviewer does not do

Read how we handle student data in the Privacy Policy.

  • It does not hire or reject. Scores support practice and coaching. Eligibility and selection stay with the institution and the company.
  • It does not share practice scores with recruiters. Results are for the student and the staff who support them.
  • It does not expose AI keys. Every AI request is made from the AlphaGrew server.
  • It does not claim to be a human interviewer. Students always know they are practising with software.

Questions

About the AI Interviewer

More in the full FAQs.

Is the interview spoken or on video?

Neither, yet. Interviews are typed in the current build. Speech transcription is in development but not yet in the interview screen, and there is no video interview.

Does the interviewer adapt in real time?

Not in the sense of live AI judgement. Between questions, a fixed rule moves difficulty one step up or down based on the last answers, and a vague answer or unchecked claim can get one follow-up question.

Which AI does it use?

AI requests are made only from the AlphaGrew server, through a provider configured for each deployment, and keys never reach the browser. Without a provider, questions come from a curated question bank and scoring uses rule-based signals. Answer quality with a live provider is still being evaluated, so ask to see it in your demo.

Are interview scores used to shortlist students?

No. Scores are for practice. They are visible to the student and to staff at their institution, not to recruiters, and they are not used to accept or reject anyone. Staff review of AI feedback is planned.

What if a resume cannot be read?

The student is told why the file could not be read and can upload another. Resume-based questions need a resume that has been read successfully; until then, practice is based on the target role.

How often can students practise?

Each student has one interview in progress at a time. How many interviews an institution can run depends on its set-up, which we agree during the pilot.
